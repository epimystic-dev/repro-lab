# Research radar - week of 2026-10-18

The first entry in repro-lab's radar. The frontier scan this week confirms the
charter's premise: reproducibility defects in the published scholarly record
live in artifacts that are already on disk, and the gap between what exists
and what can be checked offline is wide. Two artifacts were built from this
signal; the rest of the list is logged for later weeks.

## What the frontier is doing

1. **Divergent LLM evaluation scores from the same code, same model, same
   prompts.** Promptlayer's 2026 writeup documents cross-run variance driven
   by sampling, hardware, and model-version drift; LLM eval scores differ
   across GPU types and batch sizes even when every explicit input is
   identical.
   [why-llm-evaluation-results-arent-reproducible](https://www.promptlayer.com/blog/why-llm-evaluation-results-arent-reproducible-and-what-to-do-about-it/)

2. **Citation-layer hallucination studies.** `CheckIfExist` (arXiv
   2602.15871) and `HALLMARK` (arXiv 2607.18360) both diagnose the "cited
   work does not exist" failure mode of AI-generated scholarly text.
   `doi-checker` at ACM 3845864 tested a resolver pipeline across major
   conferences 2020-2026. The 2026 cohort uniformly resolves identifiers
   against live registries at scan time, which rules every one of them out
   of offline CI and air-gapped research environments.
   [CheckIfExist](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15871v1) /
   [HALLMARK](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18360) /
   [doi-checker](https://doi.org/10.1145/3844135.3845864)

3. **Seed-but-never-applied patterns in production ML code.** Three
   independently-documented 2026 incidents of the same anti-pattern:
   `aidotse/LeakPro` PR #451 added `audit.random_seed` to the schema but no
   call site; `my-monalisa-org/ai-science-target-001-protein-folding-seed-control`
   issue #1 found `np.random.normal` and `train_test_split` running with
   `config.yaml` setting `random_seed: null`; `lmstudio-ai/mlx-engine` #379
   documented `seed` ignored on the batched path.
   [LeakPro PR #451](https://github.com/aidotse/LeakPro/pull/451) /
   [mlx-engine #379](https://github.com/lmstudio-ai/mlx-engine/issues/379)

4. **Comparability of partial evaluation-harness runs.** `mlscorecheck`
   (arXiv 2311.07541) and the EvalCards framework (arXiv 2511.21695)
   both argue that eval-harness result files should be lintable for
   consistency and completeness before downstream comparison. The space
   around comparability of partial runs with mismatched few-shot or
   sampling settings is active and under-tooled.
   [mlscorecheck](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.07541) /
   [EvalCards](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21695)

5. **ML notebook reproducibility rates below 25 percent.** The 2024 review
   "Reproducibility in Machine Learning-Driven Research" (arXiv 2307.10320)
   remains the authoritative empirical baseline that the charter's claim
   rests on. The follow-up "Reproducibility in Machine Learning-based
   Research: Overview, Barriers and Drivers" (arXiv 2406.14325) extends
   the picture across barriers and drivers.
   [arXiv 2307.10320](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10320) /
   [arXiv 2406.14325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14325)

6. **Container-level reproduction leaves the "runs but figures differ"
   problem unsolved.** A notebook that executes end-to-end under a frozen
   container may still produce low output fidelity; the defect lives in
   seed and sampling discipline, not dependency pins.

7. **Testing performance-score consistency across published tables.**
   Toth and Fazekas (arXiv 2310.12527) formalise the question of whether
   numbers in a published results table are internally consistent with
   the metric formulas they claim to compute. The paper argues this
   should be a cheap pre-publication check.
   [arXiv 2310.12527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.12527)

## What we chose to build and why

The charter has a 10-candidate pool. The frontier scan concentrates signal on
three of them this week: `doicheck`, `seedaudit`, and `tablecheck`. We built
the first two.

**`doicheck`** (built, kind=linter, 15 rules, 67 tests green). The 2026
citation-hallucination wave produced excellent *resolver* tools; it did not
produce an offline-only, zero-network format and checksum validator. That
gap is a strict subset of the problem and strictly easier to meet
world-class on than a resolver. Scope: DOI / arXiv / ORCID / ISBN format,
checksums (ORCID ISO 7064 Mod 11,2; ISBN-10 mod-11; ISBN-13 mod-10),
BibTeX hygiene (required fields, duplicate keys, future years, empty
values, unbalanced braces). Clean-room from the public specs; no reference
implementation consulted. A clean run means the record is well-formed, not
that the identifiers resolve.

**`seedaudit`** (built, kind=linter, 7 rules, 35 tests green). The
seed-but-never-applied pattern is the most frequent documented reproducibility
defect of 2026. A grep-based check misses alias-resolved call sites; an
import-trace is overkill. The AST middle ground - resolve aliases, walk
scopes, flag unseeded RNG calls, flag reseed-mid-function and
seed-declared-but-unused - is cheap, exact, and dependency-free. Scope:
stdlib `random`, `numpy.random`, PyTorch, TensorFlow, scikit-learn splits.
Clean-room from each framework's public documentation.

**`tablecheck`** was considered but deferred. The task is well-posed (check
numbers in a README table against the results file the table claims to
summarise) but has an ingestion problem - every lab serialises results
differently. We would need to pick a canonical "harness result file"
shape before building, and `evalrunlint` (candidate 1 in the charter) is
the right prior step. Scheduled for a later week.
